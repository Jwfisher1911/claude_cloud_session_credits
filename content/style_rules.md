# VERC Writing Style

The generator checks every draft against these rules. Drafts that break them get one automatic revision.

## Do
- Use clear, simple, spartan language.
- Use short, direct sentences.
- Use active voice.
- Give practical, actionable steps.
- Address the reader as "you" and "your".
- Support claims with examples. Use numbers only from the approved source files or from clearly labeled examples.
- Write instructions as commands: "Open your calendar," not "You can open your calendar."

## Do not use
- Em dashes anywhere. Use periods and commas.
- Semicolons.
- Adverbs.
- Hashtags or emojis.
- Metaphors and clichés. Military planning terms (battle rhythm, first light, after-action review) are terms, not metaphors, and are allowed.
- Generalizations.
- "Not just X but Y" constructions.
- Setup phrases: in conclusion, in closing, in summary, moreover, furthermore, however, hence.

## Banned words
can, may, just, that, very, really, literally, actually, certainly, probably, basically, could, maybe, delve, embark, enlightening, esteemed, shed light, craft, crafting, imagine, realm, game-changer, unlock, discover, skyrocket, skyrocketing, abyss, not alone, in a world where, revolutionize, disruptive, utilize, utilizing, dive deep, tapestry, illuminate, unveil, pivotal, intricate, elucidate, harness, exciting, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, remarkable, remains to be seen, glimpse into, navigating, landscape, stark, testament, boost, opened up, powerful, inquiries, ever-evolving

## Replacements that keep sentences clean
- "can" or "could": use "will", a direct command, or rewrite the sentence.
- "that": drop it ("the task you choose") or use "which" or "this".
- "may" or "maybe": use "might" only when needed, or state the fact.
- "however": start a new sentence with "But".
- "discover": use "find".
- "utilize": use "use".
- "boost": use "raise" or "increase".
