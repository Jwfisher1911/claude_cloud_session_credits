# Daily Battle Drills Workbook

## How to use this workbook

- Print one copy of each worksheet. Copy Worksheet 1 for each working day of the week.
- Use a pen. Write by hand. Keep the pages in one folder or binder.
- Follow the order of the lesson guide. Fill out the card, build the grid, track the numbers, and review on Friday.
- Bring Worksheet 3 to TM 1-06. You will use your numbers when you define who you serve and what you sell.

## Worksheet 1: Daily MET Card

Use this card to set tomorrow's first-light task, your three Mission-Essential Tasks, and your admin list below the line.

1. Fill out this card at the end of each working day for the next day.
2. Write one first-light task. Name the person, the action, and the time. Keep it under 30 minutes.
3. Write three METs in priority order.
4. Mark each revenue-touching task with an R in the R column. At least one task carries an R.
5. List admin below the line.
6. At the end of the day, check off finished tasks. Copy any unfinished MET onto tomorrow's card. It takes one of tomorrow's three slots.

## Worksheet 2: Weekly Battle Rhythm Planner

Use this grid to assign each half-day of your week to revenue, delivery, financial review, or rest.

1. Write the week's start date.
2. Mark your rest day. Pick at least one full day.
3. Mark at least two half-day revenue blocks. Pick times when your customers answer the phone.
4. Mark your delivery blocks. Group paid work together.
5. Mark one financial review block. Friday afternoon pairs with your after-action review.
6. Post the grid where you see it each morning.
7. Hold this rhythm for four weeks before you change it.

## Worksheet 3: Four-Numbers Tracker

Use this tracker to record contacts made, offers made, invoices sent, and money collected each working day for four weeks.

1. Write your definition of each number before you start counting.
2. Record the four numbers at the end of each working day. This takes two minutes.
3. Write zero when the count is zero. Do not skip a day.
4. Total each column on Friday for your financial review and AAR.
5. Use the pattern guide to find where your sales process stalls.

### Pattern guide

| Pattern | What it means | MET to add |
|---|---|---|
| Many contacts, few offers | Conversations stop before you name a price | Name a price in your next conversations |
| Many offers, few invoices | Customers decline, or work sits unfinished | Follow up on open offers, finish open work |
| Many invoices, low collections | Billing follow-up has a gap | Call each client with an open invoice |

## Worksheet 4: Friday After-Action Review Form

Use this form each Friday to compare your plan with what happened and pick one improvement for next week.

1. Block 30 minutes on Friday afternoon, after your financial review.
2. Gather your MET cards, weekly grid, four-numbers tracker, and last week's AAR.
3. Answer the five questions in writing. State facts. Skip blame.
4. Write one improvement as a task with a day and a time.
5. Put the improvement on next week's grid.
6. File this form in your folder or binder.
